## The constraint that shaped this list

The registry assigns StampMyPlans "contractor direct response and contractor
process content ONLY". That rules out the obvious angles here: a piece aimed at
roofing contractors about their submittal process, or at installers about
turnaround, belongs to that brand no matter how well it would perform.

So every piece below is framed for the **homeowner or the ordering party**, the
audience this brand owns. Where a contractor is in the story they are a
character rather than the reader. Two candidates were dropped for failing this
test and are listed at the end so nobody re-proposes them.

## What this proposal cannot tell you

Stated plainly rather than papered over:

- **"What ranks today and why it is beatable" is not answered.** That needs a
  `serp-overview` pull, which has not been authorised or made. The beatability
  column below carries measured KD where the batch returned it and says so where
  it did not.
- **Six of the eight have no measured volume of their own.** They are supporting
  depth around two head terms that do, which is what "depth over breadth" means.
  Proposing them as volume plays would be inventing numbers.

### Depth on what already exists, no new URLs

**7. FAQ expansion on both money service pages**

The batch returned a long tail of real questions at zero measured volume:
how long a certification stays good for, whether the lender schedules the
inspection, whether a waiver exists, whether Fannie Mae requires one on all
manufactured home loans, expiration behaviour. These are genuine questions and
they are **not pages**. Zero volume means they do not carry a URL; it does not
mean nobody asks.

Add them as FAQ entries on the two service pages, which grows FAQPage schema
coverage and depth without minting thin pages. Roughly six to eight new
questions per page.

**8. Deepen `/industries/lenders-and-loan-officers`**

Lenders are where the measured volume actually lives, and that page currently
treats manufactured home certification as one of four services. Give it a
manufactured-home-specific section: the condition language underwriters use, the
clerical fields that cause reissues, and the parallel-versus-sequential timing
point that the Harlingen page makes and this page does not.
